Day 3 of the Ten Supreme Virtues

Uttam Arjav: The Jain Virtue of Supreme Straightforwardness

Uttam Arjav, or Supreme Straightforwardness, is presented as the third of the Ten Supreme Virtues (Das Lakshan Dharma) observed during the Jain festival of Paryushan or Daslakshan Parva. At its heart is a simple but demanding principle: there should be honesty and harmony between what we think, what we say, and what we do.

What Is Uttam Arjav?

The presentation describes Uttam Arjav as the virtue of honesty, simplicity, and the absence of deceit (Maya) in thought, speech, and action. Straightforwardness is not merely about speaking plainly. It is about removing the gap between the inner self and the outward personality.

A person practicing Arjav does not deliberately create one impression while secretly thinking or intending something else. Instead, thoughts, words, and actions move in the same direction. This alignment creates a life that is less complicated by concealment, manipulation, and artificial appearances.

The central idea: Uttam Arjav means bringing one's thoughts, speech, and actions into a single, coherent stream, without the crookedness of deceit or duplicity.

The Threefold Alignment of Arjav

One of the core components to be highlighted is the alignment of threefold actions. Straightforwardness asks us to examine three connected layers of our conduct.

Thoughts What we genuinely think and intend within ourselves.
Speech What we communicate through our words.
Actions What we actually do in the world.

When these three are harmonious, a person's conduct becomes transparent and consistent. When they diverge, there can be room for pretense: saying one thing, intending another, and ultimately doing something different.

Arjav and the Absence of Deceit

The presentation places Maya, or deceit, at the center of what Arjav seeks to overcome. It describes Arjav as the opposite of crookedness, hypocrisy, and cunning behavior.

Deceit can make a person's external behavior appear different from the reality within. Someone may hide a mistake, construct a false image, manipulate a situation, or speak in a way designed to produce an impression that is not genuine. Arjav calls for moving away from this pattern and toward transparent conduct.

In this sense, straightforwardness is not simply a communication technique. It is an inner discipline: the effort to reduce the distance between who we are, what we intend, what we say, and what we do.

Transparency: Living Without Hidden Motives

Another important aspect identified in the slides is transparency. A straightforward person acts without hidden agendas or ulterior motives. The emphasis is therefore not only on avoiding explicit lies but also on examining the intention behind one's behavior.

This makes Arjav an inward-looking virtue. A person can appear honest on the surface while still carrying a concealed intention. Practicing Arjav requires attention to that inner layer as well.

Why Is Arjav Connected With Purification of the Soul?

The presentation explains that deceit is a major source of negative karmic bondage and that eliminating deceit helps purify the soul. From this perspective, straightforwardness is part of the movement toward spiritual purification.

The slides also describe Arjav as a remedy for the Maya passion—the deceptive tendency that makes the soul "crooked" and binds it to the cycle of rebirth. Removing deceit therefore has a spiritual purpose beyond social honesty: it is connected with the effort to return to the soul's natural simplicity.

Practical Application of Uttam Arjav

The virtue becomes meaningful when it is translated into everyday conduct. The presentation identifies three practical expressions of Arjav.

1. Honest Conduct: Admit Mistakes Openly

Practicing Arjav means being willing to acknowledge mistakes rather than hiding them. Concealing an error may protect an image temporarily, but it also creates another layer of complication: the need to maintain the concealment.

Honest conduct takes the simpler path. If a mistake has occurred, acknowledge it clearly and allow the situation to be addressed without constructing a false explanation.

2. No Duplicity: Do Not Say One Thing and Do Another

Straightforwardness requires consistency between words and behavior. Saying one thing while doing another creates a gap between speech and action—the very gap that Arjav seeks to remove.

The practical question is straightforward: Does my behavior reflect what I have said and what I genuinely intend?

3. Living Simply: Avoid Manipulative Strategies

The presentation associates Arjav with living with the sincerity of a child, free from complicated and manipulative strategies. Simplicity here means reducing the need to construct elaborate stories, masks, or strategies to control how others perceive us.

The fewer false appearances a person maintains, the less effort is required to preserve them. Straightforwardness therefore brings a kind of inner simplicity.

Arjav and Trust in Relationships

Straightforward conduct also has an interpersonal dimension. The presentation notes that a person who practices Arjav earns respect, trust, and inner peace.

Trust becomes possible when people experience consistency. If someone's words repeatedly correspond with their conduct, others have less reason to search for hidden motives or conflicting intentions. In this way, Arjav can make relationships simpler and more transparent.

The Relationship Between the First Three Tenets

One of the most important ideas in the presentation is that the first three virtues of the sequence are not treated as isolated qualities. They are described as building upon one another like a spiritual ladder.

1. Uttam Kshama — Forgiveness: The Foundation

The first step is Uttam Kshama, or Forgiveness. The presentation explains that before a person can be genuinely honest, there must be peace within. Anger can turn straightforwardness into a weapon or a defensive lie.

Practicing forgiveness helps remove the anger that may otherwise cause a person to act deceitfully out of fear, hurt, or spite. In this framing, inner peace creates the foundation on which straightforward conduct can develop.

2. Uttam Mardav — Humility: The Bridge

The second step is Uttam Mardav, or Humility. The presentation connects deceit with ego (Mana). A person may lie or behave "crookedly" because they want to appear better, richer, stronger, or more powerful than they believe they are.

Humility reduces the need for that false front. When the ego's demand to appear superior is softened, there is less reason to maintain an illusion about oneself.

3. Uttam Arjav — Straightforwardness: The Result

The third step is Uttam Arjav. The presentation summarizes the sequence by connecting each virtue with a corresponding inner obstacle and an internal shift:

Tenet Vice It Counters Internal Shift
Kshama Krodh (Anger) "I am at peace with others."
Mardav Mana (Pride) "I have no need to be superior."
Arjav Maya (Deceit) "I am exactly who I appear to be."

The sequence is therefore presented as progressive. Kshama addresses anger and establishes peace. Mardav addresses pride and loosens the need for a superior image. With anger and pride reduced, Arjav represents the soul settling into straightforwardness rather than relying on deception.

In one line: If Kshama is the peace that removes anger, and Mardav is the humility that removes the need for superiority, Arjav is the straightforwardness that removes the need for deception.

From Performance to a Natural State of Being

A particularly significant distinction in the presentation is between performing honesty and allowing honesty to become natural. It explains that Arjav is not merely a performance that depends on having first learned how to appear honest.

Without forgiveness and humility, honesty can remain superficial. A person may still be driven by anger or ego and may therefore find reasons to conceal, manipulate, or present a carefully constructed image.

But when anger is released through Kshama and the demand for superiority is softened through Mardav, straightforwardness can become less forced. Arjav is presented as the natural state that emerges from this internal progression.

Simple Questions for Practicing Arjav

The ideas in the presentation can be turned into a simple self-reflection practice. At the end of a conversation, decision, or difficult interaction, one can ask:

  • Did I say what I genuinely meant?
  • Did my actions match my words?
  • Was I hiding a mistake because I was afraid of how I would appear?
  • Was there a hidden motive behind what I said or did?
  • Was I trying to create an image of myself that was different from reality?
  • Could I have handled the situation more simply and sincerely?

These questions are not about judging oneself harshly. They are a way of noticing the places where thought, speech, intention, and action have become misaligned.

Uttam Arjav in Everyday Life

Straightforwardness can be practiced in ordinary situations: admitting when we are wrong, communicating our real intentions, avoiding unnecessary manipulation, keeping our promises aligned with our behavior, and resisting the temptation to create a more impressive version of ourselves.

Its simplicity is also its challenge. It is often easier to construct an explanation than to acknowledge a mistake, easier to protect an image than to be vulnerable, and easier to manipulate a situation than to communicate directly. Arjav asks us to choose the simpler relationship between inner intention and outward conduct.

The Spiritual Significance of Straightforwardness

In the framework presented here, Uttam Arjav is ultimately concerned with the purification of the soul. Deceit is understood not simply as a social problem but as a form of inner crookedness associated with Maya. Straightforwardness works in the opposite direction: removing deception and allowing the soul's natural simplicity to become more visible.

This is why the virtue is placed after forgiveness and humility in the sequence discussed in the presentation. Peace makes honesty possible; humility reduces the need to maintain a superior image; and straightforwardness removes the masks that remain.

Conclusion

Uttam Arjav is the practice of living without crookedness or deceit. It asks for consistency between thought, speech, and action; honesty about mistakes; freedom from duplicity; transparency of intention; and a simpler way of relating to others.

The presentation's progression from Kshama to Mardav to Arjav gives the virtue a deeper context. Forgiveness addresses anger, humility addresses pride, and straightforwardness addresses deceit. As these inner obstacles are reduced, honesty does not have to remain a carefully maintained performance. It can become a natural way of being.

In that sense, Arjav is more than "telling the truth." It is the effort to become inwardly and outwardly aligned—to live without the complicated web of false appearances and hidden motives, and to allow simplicity and sincerity to guide thought, speech, and action.
