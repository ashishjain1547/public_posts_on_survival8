The Day My Poem About Self-Love Went Viral -- And Nearly Destroyed Me

How a message that reached 250 million people taught me that self-love is not a switch you flick on, but a relationship you build one small choice at a time.

The Empty Feeling We All Know

There is a hollow place in the chest that so many of us walk around with every single day. It feels like a quiet ache, a background hum of not-enoughness. And for the longest time, I assumed that ache was a signal. A sign that I was missing something out there. A person. A title. A product. A destination. Something that, once acquired, would finally make the emptiness go away.

That is exactly what a consumerist society wants you to believe. The entire machinery of modern marketing runs on a single, devastating premise: you are not enough. Buy this car and you will be desirable. Buy this skincare line and you will be beautiful. Buy this program and you will be successful. The message is woven so seamlessly into our daily lives that we stop noticing it. We start absorbing it as truth.

And here is the cruel irony. The makeup they sell to make you feel prettier is the same makeup you buy to stop feeling worse about the lie they keep whispering in your ear. The lie that you are not enough. It is a closed loop. A cycle that keeps you buying, keeps you chasing, and keeps you hollow.

A Message in the Mirror

What if the answer was never out there? What if the person you have been searching for has been with you the entire time?

That is the core message that, years ago, I put into a poem. A poem that would eventually be viewed more than 250 million times. The central line was simple: "You are who you have been looking for." The idea was that the emptiness comes not from a lack of external things, but from a fractured relationship with the person in the mirror. We keep looking outward for validation, love, and completion, when the only person who can truly make us happy is already here.

In that poem, I also shared a piece of advice that a friend gave me at a pivotal moment. I used to be quick to defend her when she spoke negatively about herself. If she said, "I'm so dumb," I would say, "You're brilliant." If she said, "I feel ugly," I would say, "You look beautiful." One day she turned to me and asked why I was so dutifully filling up her cup while treating my own cup so irresponsibly. Then she gave me a hug and said: "Treat yourself like someone you love."

That sentence rearranged something in me.

When the World Listens

When the poem went viral, it happened almost overnight. I woke up one morning in Bali -- because that is where you go when you are healing from a breakup and living like a hippie -- and my phone would not stop buzzing. Thousands upon thousands of new followers. The poem had not just gone viral. It had gone mega viral. Forty million views in the first forty-eight hours. More than a million views a day for months.

With that kind of reach came an avalanche of messages. Most of them were beautiful. People said the poem moved them. They sent it to their mothers, their brothers, their partners. They said it changed their lives.

But for every nine messages of appreciation, there was one that was very different.

"I don't know how to love myself. Can you help me?"

"My husband hits me and I'm too scared to leave. Can you help me?"

"I'm thinking of killing myself tomorrow. Can you help me?"

And I couldn't.

I had a poem that was reaching millions of people, but I did not have a real answer for the people who needed something more than poetry. I felt like a fraud. The shame and guilt were so heavy that my body began to break down. I found myself locked in a dark hotel room, feverish, shivering, unable to open the curtains because even the smallest amount of light felt excruciating. That moment was the beginning of a multi-year descent into deep depression -- a quest to find the answer I did not yet have.

The Wrong Way to Seek Self-Love

I did what any desperate person would do. I tried everything.

Meditation. Journaling. Writing poetry. Singing. Dancing. Cold plunges. Saunas. Baths. Hiring coaches. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy. Internal Family Systems. Trauma Release Exercises. Neuro-Linguistic Programming. Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing. If it had an acronym, I probably did it. Detoxes. Juice cleanses. Water fasts. Psychic readings. Astrology. Tarot. Plant medicine ceremonies deep in the Amazon jungle. Punching pillows. Primal screaming. And yes, naked mirror work -- standing in front of a mirror repeating "I love you" over and over again, because that is what you do when you are desperate and a hippie.

Here is the problem. So much of the self-love content I consumed at the time spoke about self-love like it was a switch. You either have it or you do not. One day it clicks on, and suddenly you are whole. So I kept doing all of these practices, waiting for that single moment when my self-love would finally activate.

But it never did.

Instead, I saw a small shift. And then another. And then another. Tiny, almost imperceptible changes that accumulated over time. And that is when I began to understand something crucial: self-love is not a switch. It is a result. It is the byproduct of how we show up for ourselves each and every day.

Self-Love Is a Relationship, Not a Switch

Think about any healthy relationship in your life. It is not built in a day. It is not built through one grand gesture. It is built through thousands of small, consistent acts of care and attention over time. So why would the relationship you have with yourself be any different?

The challenge is that, because the relationship with yourself is internal, most of us have no idea where to start. That is where a framework developed by Dr. Gary Chapman comes in. You may have heard of the Five Love Languages. They are typically used to deepen connection in romantic relationships, but they apply just as powerfully to the relationship you have with yourself. The five categories are: acts of service, quality time, physical touch, gifts, and words of affirmation.

Let me show you how each one can be turned inward.

Love Language How We Typically Misuse It with Ourselves How to Actually Practice It
Acts of Service Overworking, people-pleasing, or avoiding our own needs entirely Doing something kind for your future self: meal prepping, scheduling that appointment, tidying your space
Quality Time Being alone while endlessly scrolling on your phone, numbing out Genuinely being present with yourself: journaling, a walk without headphones, sitting with your thoughts
Physical Touch Neglecting the body, treating it roughly, or only using touch as performance Stretching, massage, placing a hand on your chest, hugging yourself, caring for your skin
Gifts Retail therapy that leaves you feeling emptier, or denying yourself anything nice Intentional gifts to yourself: a book, a tool for a hobby, something that honors your interests
Words of Affirmation Constant negative self-talk: "I'm so dumb," "I'll never succeed," "I'm not good enough" Speaking to yourself the way you would speak to someone you deeply love and respect

Take quality time as an example. How much connection actually happens when two people sit together but both are on their phones? Very little. And yet, how often do we do the exact same thing by ourselves? We confuse being alone with spending quality time with ourselves. We drown out our inner voice with mindless scrolling and then wonder why we feel disconnected from ourselves.

Or consider words of affirmation. How would you feel if someone followed you around all day saying, "You're so stupid. You're not good enough. You're going to fail." You would be devastated. You would probably cut that person out of your life. And yet, many of us say those exact words to ourselves every single day without even noticing.

Attention and Intention: The Two Keys

The good news is that this can change. The change begins with two things: attention and intention.

When you use the Five Love Languages as a direction, you can point your attention toward your relationship with yourself. You start to catch those little moments when you are not being loving to yourself. You notice the harsh words. You notice the neglected needs. You notice the patterns.

Then, when you combine that new awareness with the intention to love yourself more deeply, you begin to unlock new choices. Choices you could not even see before. Ways to strengthen that relationship, one small act at a time.

And here is what I learned: when you start making those new choices, the universe starts giving you your next step. Sometimes that next step is enormous and terrifying -- leaving a toxic relationship, quitting a soul-crushing job, finally starting therapy. But often, it is a very small step. Equally important, but small. Writing a poem. Finally reading a book on self-compassion. Taking five minutes to breathe.

The point is that you do not need to know the entire path. You only need to take the next step that is in front of you.

Finding Heaven in the Ordinary

I eventually wrote a second poem. I called it "Heaven." And the message was very different from the first.

The first poem was about self-love as an identity -- you are already enough. The second poem was about self-love as a lived experience -- the willingness to feel everything, to embrace the full spectrum of being human.

The most impactful thing you can do for the world, I believe, is to learn how to love life. All of life. Not just the pretty parts. Not just the highlight reel. All of it. Show people that being happy does not mean turning a blind eye to pain and sadness and suffering. Show them that choosing love does not mean ignoring fear. It just means being willing to feel.

Because the fear of feeling is at the root of every fear. It creates the fear of living, because feeling is why we are here. Your emotions are not your enemy. They are the gift. Energy in motion, with the strength to bring a king to his knees -- and yet contained within emotion is the key to set you free.

This is the shift from scarcity to abundance. It is not about what you have. It is about what you see. When you only focus on what you lack, you cannot appreciate what you have. But with gratitude for what you have, you begin to see that it is a lot. The next time you forget how blessed you are, take a moment and focus on the next breath you breathe. Notice that you are here. A miracle. A great mystery. Out of hundreds of trillions of stars and hundreds of billions of galaxies, somehow, someway, you are here today.

You were once one of 200 million sperm. You happened to get there first. How could you ever say you are cursed? You have been blessed since before your birth.

Perspective: The Antidote to Self-Pity

This is not toxic positivity. I am not saying that life is fair or that it is easy. We all go through a lot. But as you walk through the valley of the shadow of death, do not lose gratitude for the fact that you walk. Celebrate the fact that you can dance and sing. Embrace the abundance in the very act of speaking, of tasting, of touching, of breathing.

There are people with anosmia, a condition where the sense of smell is completely lost. They literally cannot stop and smell the roses. And yet some of us complain that we do not have time to stop at all. There are people who would trade places with you without a second thought. No matter how many tears, heartbreaks, or bad days you are going through, the cemetery is full of souls that know living is worth the cost.

But make no mistake: being alive is not the same thing as living. Just like throwing something away is not the same thing as giving. If you have not yet found your love of life, I suggest you keep digging. Allow yourself to feel your way through all the hurt and all the rage. And as you feel, allow yourself to finally turn the page. That is how we heal. It might hurt like hell, but you will have shown the rest of us that it is possible to feel it all and still love life. That is heaven. And it is available right now.

Key Takeaways

If you take nothing else from this piece, take these truths:

- You are already enough. The emptiness you feel is not evidence of your inadequacy. It is evidence of a fractured relationship with yourself.

- Self-love is not a switch. It is a result. It is the byproduct of showing up for yourself consistently, with attention and intention.

- Use the Five Love Languages as a compass. Acts of service, quality time, physical touch, gifts, and words of affirmation can all be turned inward. Start with one.

- Catch your negative self-talk. Speak to yourself the way you would speak to someone you deeply love.

- Take the next step, no matter how small. The universe is always offering you a clue. You do not need to see the whole staircase.

- Gratitude is the gateway to abundance. It is not about what you have. It is about what you see.

- Feeling is not weakness. The willingness to feel everything is the highest form of strength, and it is the path to loving life itself.

Final Thoughts

For all the people who reached out to me years ago asking for an answer, I am sorry I did not have it then. But I want to say thank you. Thank you for helping me find it so that I could share it with all the people who need it today.

If you are one of those people -- if you are in the darkness right now -- I want you to know that it is possible to get out. One hundred percent possible. And even if it takes years, it is so worth it. I know because I did it. Day by day, the voices in my head got a little less mean. I still get sad sometimes, but I show up for myself completely differently when I do. I have learned to relate differently to my own emotions, to other people, and to life itself.

I learned how to love myself. And just like the poem said would happen, my world got brighter and my load got lighter.

That is something worth writing poetry about.
